# Evaluating product animations

Technical eligibility and taste are separate results. The physical product is the visual reference; simulator footage supports comparison and records its own confidence.

## Reproducible capture

Obtain an explicit device/camera handoff from the active test owner. Record firmware identity, product/profile, physical LED count, output order, scene type and controls, palette, master and automatic brightness, audio source, camera viewpoint, exposure, white balance and capture time. Fix camera exposure and white balance when supported. Save the original scene/configuration and restore it after the session. A changed viewpoint or automatic exposure lowers comparison confidence.

Use a fixed palette and brightness for the first comparison, then each scene's intended settings. Record at least 30 seconds and two complete cycles for deterministic motion. Twinkles and other stochastic scenes need several samples. Audio scenes use the same input excerpt; label simulated audio. For a shortlist, add front and oblique views, dim-room and normal-room runs, and a sustained observation covering control and persistence writes.

## Technical result

Record pass, fail or untested for full intended pixel coverage, unintended flashes, colour/white-channel output, frame pacing, stable heap over repeated transitions, reconnect, and restoration of the original scene. Video candidate detection is a review aid: compare suspicious frames with source preview and intended audio before calling a flash a defect. A short clean clip does not prove long-run stability. A technical failure stays visible beside the rating rather than becoming a low aesthetic score.

## Taste dimensions

Rate each axis 1–5 with a short observation; use `unrated` for missing evidence. A 3 is clear but ordinary, and a 5 is especially convincing in that dimension.

| Axis | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Apparent volume | Clearly one flat strip | Light seems distributed through the sphere |
| Hidden wiring | Individual strip/sequence dominates | Geometry is hard to infer from the motion |
| Motion and choreography | Accidental or monotonous movement | Coherent layers, counter-motion or evolving trajectories |
| Colour composition | Muddy or abrupt transitions | Clear palette relationships and useful contrast |
| Temporal variety | Immediate obvious repetition | Changes reward watching without becoming arbitrary |
| Ambient comfort | Distracting glare or nervous changes | Pleasant sustained viewing at the tested brightness |
| Party presence | Motion disappears in the setting | Readable, engaging motion from several positions |

Keep the axes independent: strong chasers may score highly for motion and presence while revealing the strip. Sparse irregular twinkles may hide wiring and suggest volume while remaining quiet. Neither trade-off is a defect.

## Preference profiles and ranking

Veit's initial profile gives apparent volume, hidden wiring and choreography weight 2; other axes weight 1. This is a provisional preference profile, not a universal quality score. Keep the raw axes and offer separate ambient and party shortlists. Use a weighted mean only when every weighted axis is rated; attach capture confidence and technical eligibility separately.

Compare promising scenes in randomized A/B pairs at matched settings. Record the preferred scene, reason, observer and context; allow ties. Initial candidates include Color Twinkles, simultaneous opposite-direction chasers, slow layered patterns, irregular particles and spatial waves. Compare current upstream effects with preserved HELIO scenes before retiring any port. User ratings can change the profile without changing the technical verdict.

## Evidence record

Each result names the clip(s), source-frame receipt, settings snapshot, technical outcomes, seven axis scores with reasons, evaluator, preference profile and confidence. A score is an interpretation of recorded output, not a physical measurement. No ranking is published for an unseen or interrupted case.
